ABOUT / SEGFAULT.GARDEN

CODE IS HEREDITY. EXECUTION IS SURVIVAL.
SEGFAULT.GARDEN is a tiny artificial-life experiment disguised as a programming game. You write the instructions for a digital organism, release it into a world of limited resources, and watch what survives.

What am I looking at?

The central canvas is a living 2,048 × 2,048 grid. Every colored pixel is a programmable organism. Green pixels are food. Organisms execute a small assembly-like language, spend energy to act, and die when they run out. Their descendants can inherit altered code. Colors distinguish genetic lineages, so zooming out reveals entire populations spreading, competing, and disappearing.

How to play

  1. Choose a genome. Select Forager, Predator, Code parasite, Self-repair, or Fast replicator, then click LOAD. Or edit the program yourself.
  2. Press RUN. Each simulated cycle executes instructions and advances the ecosystem. Adjust Speed to accelerate it, or use STEP to inspect behavior.
  3. Watch the population. Organisms spend energy, search for food, reproduce, attack, or alter neighboring code depending on their instructions.
  4. Zoom way out. Click MASSIVE ZOOM OUT to see the entire world. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and use the minimap to jump around.
  5. Inspect the survivors. Click a colored cell to see its registers, program counter, genome, generation, and ancestry. TRACE LINEAGE shows its lineage chain in the event log.
  6. Experiment. Change the genome, click INJECT INTO WORLD to introduce competitors, or start the ONE-BYTE CHALLENGE to pit mutated variants against one another.

Why does the code matter?

There is no player-controlled character. Your program is the character. A short loop may reproduce efficiently, while a complex strategy might burn energy faster than it can replenish it. FORK creates offspring; ATTACK steals energy; WRITE can rewrite a neighbor’s instruction; GUARD restores inherited code. Mutations during reproduction can change what future generations do.

Evolution, not magic

Nothing here is powered by AI or a language model. The interesting behavior comes from explicit rules: instruction execution, resource limits, reproduction, mutation, and competition. A successful lineage is not necessarily intelligent; it is simply one that happens to persist under these conditions. This is a toy artificial-life model, not a scientific simulation of biological evolution.

Try changing the order of operations, reducing the number of instructions, or switching to a more aggressive preset. See whether the resulting population grows, stalls, or collapses. The instruction reference is in the left panel; the presets are the easiest starting point.

What does the one-byte challenge do?

It creates competing, single-instruction mutations from a shared starting genome, gives them equal starting energy, and lets the world determine which lineages remain. The leaderboard, population chart, and fossil record make their fortunes visible. This is a local experiment, not an online multiplayer tournament.

Saving and replaying

SAVE STATE downloads a snapshot that LOAD STATE can restore. EXPORT RESULTS downloads the population history and lineage statistics. The simulation uses a seeded pseudorandom generator, making runs reproducible under the same starting state and settings.

Runs entirely in your browser · No backend · No account · No AI · No external dependencies. Designed as a playful experiment in computation, emergence, and evolution.
